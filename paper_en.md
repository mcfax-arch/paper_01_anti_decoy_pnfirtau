# Attention with a Calibrated Off Switch: Causal FIR Key Smoothing, the Score-Shift Pathology, and Selective Silence in Rectified Attention (`pn_fir_tau`)

**Positioning.** The silent-off-switch mechanism itself is prior art \u2014 Rectified Linear Attention (ReLA, Zhang et al.\ 2021 [11]) replaces Softmax with ReLU and its heads \u201clearn to attend to nothing\u201d; Softpick (Zuhri et al.\ 2025 [12]) scales a rectified, non-sum-to-one normalizer to 340M\u20131.8B models, removes attention sinks and massive activations, and wins at low-precision quantization; Wang (2026 [13]) frames abstention and noise filtering as the two missing primitives of Softmax attention. The open problem addressed here is therefore not whether attention can be silent \u2014 prior art answers that \u2014 but whether a rectified normalizer survives causal key smoothing. This paper positions its contributions as: causal FIR key binding (decoy robustness and single-layer induction), the diagnosis and single-threshold calibration of the score-shift pathology that rectified attention contracts under key smoothing, benchmark audits, and a fully traceable measurement record.

## Abstract

Softmax attention cannot say "nothing found": a simplex-constrained head must distribute $1.000$ units of context weight even when no key matches the query, and pre-trained models pay with attention sinks, massive outliers that degrade low-bit quantization [8, 12, 14], and RAG-style hallucinations on unanswerable queries. Rectified normalizers remove the simplex constraint — ReLA replaces Softmax with ReLU and heads "attend to nothing" [11]; Softpick scales rectified normalization to 340M–1.8B with 0% sink rate [12] — so the off switch itself is prior art. The open question is the interaction layer: A parameter-free causal FIR low-pass filter (7 half-Gaussian taps, $\sigma=2.0$) applied strictly to the key stream $K$ lets one layer bind a queried key onto the adjacent value slot, lifting non-bijective single-layer associative recall from $44.5\% \pm 0.9\%$ to $100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ and removing exact-decoy vulnerability on the legacy benchmark ($\Delta = 0.000$ at decoy noise $\epsilon \in \{0.05, 0.2, 0.5, 1.0\}$ for `sm_fir`). The same smoothing shifts scores downward and starves rectification: fixed-$\tau{=}0$ power-norm falls to $64.4\% \pm 6.8\%$ with $91.3\% \pm 8.6\%$ negative scores and $44.5\% \pm 23.0\%$ dead rows. Softmax masks this shift (translation-invariant); rectification does not (Proposition 1). One learnable threshold $\tau$ per layer (`pn_fir_tau`) restores $100.0\%$ recall and keeps unanswerable rows silent under pure cross-entropy ($\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$ vs. $0.100 \pm 0.005$ for virtual sinks), reaches matched-precision WikiText-2 parity on 2,400 steps (within $0.3\%$) and a matched-precision 50k win consistent across 3 seeds ($-2.22 \pm 0.46$ val / $-2.33 \pm 0.49$ test PPL). We release fused Triton kernels (no `exp()`, $O(N)$ attention VRAM) and a full measurement record, including an instrumented retraction of our own earlier latency claim.

## 1. Introduction: Practical Motivation and Related Work

Why modify the core attention mechanism of a Transformer? In real-world sequence modeling, standard scaled dot-product Softmax attention [2] forces practitioners to pay a steep architectural and computational tax for two basic operations: **(A) reading the value token that follows a matched key in context** (in-context learning and associative lookup), and **(B) doing nothing when a specialized attention head finds no relevant match in the context** (residual-stream preservation in RAG, tool use, and memory lookup).

### 1.1 Practical Problem 1: Compressing Two-Layer Induction Heads into a Single Layer (`sm_fir` / `pn_fir_tau`)

Associative retrieval—locating a context key $k^*$ that matches a query and routing the value $v^*$ stored at the adjacent position $j^*+1$ (`[... key A, value B ... query A] -> B`)—is the foundational mechanism of in-context learning in Large Language Models (LLMs), enabling models to copy variable names, track entity attributes, and extract facts from prompts [1].

In a standard Transformer, however, **a single attention layer is structurally incapable of content-addressable key-value induction**. Token-level dot-product attention computes similarity $S_{t,j} = \langle Q_t, K_j \rangle / \sqrt{d_k}$ strictly on a per-position basis: the query $Q_t$ (encoding the target key $k^*$) matches the key vector $K_{j^*}$ at the key position $j^*$, but the target value token $v^*$ resides at the *next* position $j^*+1$, whose unfiltered key vector $K_{j^*+1} = W_K E(v^*)$ contains zero information about $k^*$ whenever keys and values are independently paired. As proved by Olsson et al. [1], a standard Transformer must dedicate **at least two sequential layers** to solve this task: Layer 1 (*previous-token head*) must copy token $j^*$ into the residual stream of position $j^*+1$, and only in Layer 2 (*induction head*) can attention match $Q_t$ against the composite representation at $j^*+1$. In compact or edge Transformers (4–12 layers), spending early layers and residual-stream capacity simply to shift adjacent tokens is an expensive waste of depth.

A natural signal-processing remedy is to apply a fixed, parameter-free causal Finite Impulse Response (FIR) low-pass filter along the sequence axis of the key projection $K$ prior to dot-product scoring (the convolutional ancestors of this move are RWKV's learnable 2-tap *token shift* [15] on the R/K/V streams and the learned $3 \times 1$ depthwise causal convolutions of *Primer* [3]; our filter is fixed, parameter-free, applied to $K$ only, and evaluated for adversarial decoy robustness). Filtering $K$ blends the key representation of $k^*$ at position $j^*$ forward into the value position $j^*+1$:
$$\tilde{K}_{j^*+1} = h_0 K_{j^*+1} + h_1 K_{j^*} + \sum_{m=2}^{K_f-1} h_m K_{j^*+1-m}.$$
Because $\tilde{K}_{j^*+1}$ now carries a weighted component $h_1 K(k^*)$ while both the query $Q_t$ and the value stream $V_{j^*+1} = W_V E(v^*)$ remain sharp and unfiltered, **a single attention layer querying for $k^*$ can attend directly to the value slot $j^*+1$ and read out $v^*$**—lifting single-layer non-bijective associative recall from $44.5\%$ (`sm`) to **$100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$** (`sm_fir` and `pn_fir_tau`) at a latency cost of just **$+1.5\%\text{--}2.0\%$** in `sm_fir`.

### 1.2 Practical Problem 2: Preventing RAG Context Hallucinations and Eliminating Attention-Sink Outliers (`pn_fir_tau`)

In a multi-layer Transformer, the residual stream acts as a shared communication bus: every attention head reads from the residual stream and writes its output additively back into it ($y_t = x_t + \sum_{j \le t} W_{t,j} V_j$). In practice, most specialized attention heads (e.g., heads that look up a specific entity attribute, a matching syntactic bracket, or an answer in a retrieved RAG passage) are only relevant on a small fraction of tokens; on the remaining tokens—or when a retrieved RAG passage does not contain the answer to the user's query (**unanswerable query**)—the head *should stay completely silent* ($\sum_{j \le t} W_{t,j} = 0$) so that the existing residual representation $x_t$ is preserved uncorrupted.

Standard Softmax attention makes silence impossible because it enforces a strict probability simplex constraint on every query row:
$$\sum_{j=0}^{t} W_{t,j} = 1 \quad \forall t.$$
Even when no key in the context matches the query, any simplex-constrained normalizer is forced to allocate $100\%$ of its unit weight to the context. In real-world Transformers, this causes two well-documented practical pathologies:
1. **Context Hallucinations on Unanswerable Queries (RAG Corruption):** When a context document discusses a topic but lacks the specific queried fact $k^*$, a simplex-constrained head is forced to read a weighted average of the document's value vectors $V_j$ and add it to the residual stream, overwriting the model's prior state $x_t$ with a hallucinated value from the document.
2. **Attention Sinks and Massive Quantization Outliers:** To avoid corrupting the residual stream on uninformative rows, pre-trained LLMs learn an expensive workaround discovered in *Quantizable Transformers* [8] and *StreamingLLM* [9]: the model turns the initial `BOS` token at $t=0$ into an **Attention Sink** ("trash bin") by inflating hidden-state activations at $t=0$ to **massive numerical outliers** (tens to hundreds of standard deviations larger than normal tokens) whose value projection $W_V x_0 \approx 0$. These massive activation outliers [14] severely degrade **INT8/INT4 post-training quantization** [8, 12] and cause catastrophic perplexity collapse in **streaming / sliding-window inference** whenever the initial sink tokens are evicted from the KV cache [9]. Recent analysis frames exactly these two behaviours — abstention and value-pathway noise filtering — as the two missing primitives of Softmax attention [13]; this paper studies them in a thin-model regime with explicit attention-level diagnostics and refers the scale-out to that line.

In the sparse and quiet attention literature, two major families have been proposed to improve upon Softmax, and it is essential to distinguish their mathematical and practical properties:
1. **Simplex-Projected Sparse Normalizers (`Sparsemax` [4], $\alpha$-`Entmax` [5, 6]):** Given a score row $s \in \mathbb{R}^{t+1}$, Tsallis $\alpha$-Entmax ($\alpha > 1$, with $p = \frac{1}{\alpha - 1}$ so $\alpha=2 \implies p=1$ is Sparsemax and $\alpha=1.5 \implies p=2$ is 1.5-Entmax) solves the strictly convex simplex projection:
   $$W_{t,j}^{\text{entmax}}(s) = \max\!\big(s_j - \tau^*(s),\, 0\big)^p, \quad \text{where } \tau^*(s) \in \mathbb{R} \text{ is the unique root of } \sum_{j=0}^{t} \max\!\big(s_j - \tau^*(s),\, 0\big)^p = 1.$$
   Because $\tau^*(s + c\mathbf{1}) = \tau^*(s) + c$, Sparsemax and $\alpha$-Entmax are **exact translation-invariant normalizers**: adding any constant shift $c$ to a row of scores leaves the attention weights completely unchanged. However, by definition of the simplex constraint $\sum_{j=0}^{t} W_{t,j}^{\text{entmax}}(s) \equiv 1$, **Sparsemax and Entmax can never produce an all-zero row (`selective silence = 0`)**. Worse still, when a query key $k^*$ is absent from a topic-coherent document, Sparsemax and Entmax concentrate $100\%$ of their weight onto the single least-negative mismatching token rather than averaging across the document, which—as we demonstrate in Experiment 5—actually *increases* the residual-stream hallucination rate ($32.4\%\text{--}34.4\%$) compared to standard Softmax ($27.2\%$).
2. **Virtual-Sink Softmax Normalizers (`Softmax_1` / Off-by-One [7], Quiet Softmax [8], `StreamingLLM` Attention Sinks [9]):** Augmentation methods append a virtual "sink" or "null" column with fixed score $0$ (`Softmax_1`) or learnable scalar score $\tau$ (`Quiet Softmax`):
   $$W_{t,j}^{\text{sink}}(s) = \frac{\exp(s_j)}{\sum_{m=0}^{t} \exp(s_m) + \exp(\tau)}, \qquad \sum_{j=0}^{t} W_{t,j}^{\text{sink}}(s) = 1 - \frac{\exp(\tau)}{\sum_{m=0}^{t} \exp(s_m) + \exp(\tau)} < 1.$$
   While virtual sinks break shift-invariance in order to allow total context weight to drop below $1$, they remain based on the strictly positive exponential function $\exp(s_j) > 0$. Consequently, across $t+1$ context tokens, individual non-matching tokens still retain non-zero exponential tails, leaking substantial residual weight ($\sum_j W_j = 0.100 \pm 0.005$ in Experiment 5) even when no key matches.
3. **Rectified (Sub-Simplex) Normalizers — established prior art (`ReLA` [11], `Softpick` [12]):** The silence mechanism studied here is not a novelty of this paper. ReLA [11] replaces Softmax with ReLU on scorer output, observes that sparsity arises without projection and that heads "learn to attend to nothing" (their term), and stabilises training through LayerNorm with a specialised initialization or an extra gating function. Softpick [12] trains a rectified, deliberately non-sum-to-one normalizer at 340M–1.8B parameters, reports a 0% attention-sink rate, much lower hidden-state kurtosis, and better low-bit quantized accuracy than Softmax. Our `pn_fir_tau` differs from this line in a specific and falsifiable way: it couples a **per-layer learnable threshold $\tau$** with a **peak-driven silence gate**, and the threshold exists for a reason that only appears under key smoothing — the score-shift pathology of Section 2 — rather than as a stability device. ReLA's stability trick (initialisation/gating under LayerNorm) and Softpick's power choice address a related failure mode, yet neither is calibrated against the distribution shift that a causal key filter imposes; that interaction is the subject of this paper, along with the decoy-robustness audit and the residual-preservation protocol under pure cross-entropy (Exp 5).

To be explicit about the delta: the taxonomy above establishes that exact-zero-row silence was demonstrated (ReLA [11], Softpick [12]); this paper's variables are the **causal FIR key binding with decoy diagnostics**, the **score-shift pathology and its single-scalar calibration** (Proposition 1), the **residual-preservation measurement under pure cross-entropy** (Exp 5), and the **fused-kernel plus measurement-forensics layer (Exp 4/4b)** — each of which is unclaimed by prior art and each of which would need to collapse for this paper to be empty.

Given that prior art, the open question is not *whether* attention can be silent — ReLA [11] and Softpick [12] answer that — but *under what conditions a rectified normalizer survives key smoothing and calibrates exactly zero-mass silence* ($\sum_j W_j = 0.002 \pm 0.002$ in Experiment 5; $0.000 \pm 0.000$ in `pn_fir_tau_reg`) without `BOS` sink tokens or transcendental `exp()` evaluations in the kernel. That question is answered by **sub-simplex rectified power-normalization** with exponent $p=10.0$, a **learnable scalar threshold $\tau$**, and a **peak-driven silence gate** $g_t \in [0, 1)$.

### 1.3 The Score-Shift Paradox (`sm_fir` vs. `pn_fir` vs. `pn_fir_c` vs. `pn_fir_tau`)

When we evaluate the 13 candidate attention stacks from our architecture survey (Experiment 1), a striking paradox emerges:
- Adding a 7-tap causal Gaussian FIR filter on $K$ to standard Softmax (`sm` $\to$ `sm_fir`) improves clean recall from **$71.5\% \pm 1.4\%$** to **$100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$** across all random seeds (and from **$44.5\% \pm 0.9\%$** to **$100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$** on our non-bijective content-addressable benchmark in Experiment 2).
- Adding the *exact same* causal FIR filter on $K$ to uncalibrated rectified power-normalization (`pn` $\to$ `pn_fir`, fixed $\tau=0$) during positive-only retrieval training yields highly erratic, degraded performance (**$64.4\% \pm 6.8\%$** in Exp 1; **$88.8\% \pm 9.2\%$** in Exp 2), while the non-causal FFT low-pass counterpart (`pn_fft`) reaches $98.9\% \pm 0.3\%$ only by leaking future information (`past_key_leak = 0.785`).
- Attempting to restore shift-invariance via row-mean centering (`pn_fir_c`) rescues clean recall ($100.0\%$ in Exp 1; $96.5\%$ in Exp 2), but replaces threshold-based silence with dispersion-based gating: on uncalibrated noise rows (Exp 1) and ungated recall (Exp 2) it fires with $\sum_j W_j(\text{noise}) = 0.938\text{--}1.000$, and even when trained end-to-end with unanswerable queries (Exp 5) it leaks $\approx 124\times$ more unanswerable row mass ($0.265 \pm 0.045$ vs. $0.002 \pm 0.002$) while losing $10.5$ percentage points of answerable override accuracy ($88.5\%$ vs. $99.0\%$).

In this paper, we provide a complete mathematical and empirical resolution to these phenomena, accompanied by a self-contained reproducibility package (`src/`, `experiments/`, `results/`, `figures/`) in which every number and figure is generated directly from raw experimental logs.

### 1.4 Where This Work Stands: Prior-Art Map and Honest Delta

Four research lines bracket this work, and the placement defines what we claim. The *rectified silent attention* line is established prior art: ReLA [11] replaces Softmax with ReLU and reports heads that switch off; Softpick [12] scales a rectified, non-sum-to-one normalizer to 340M-1.8B parameters with a 0% sink rate and quantization gains; Wang [13] models abstention and noise filtering as the two missing primitives of Softmax attention and measures value- and query-gated mechanisms from 10M to 20B parameters. The *sink and outlier* line (attention sinks and massive activations [8, 9, 14]) motivates this study but is not a contribution of it. The *sub-simplex normalizer* is therefore not what this paper adds. What it adds is the interaction layer and the measurement layer:

(i) the causal-FIR-to-rectification interaction: a 7-tap causal key filter binds queried keys onto adjacent value slots for non-bijective recall, is immune to exact decoy injection at its design point, but shifts the score distribution downward and starves uncalibrated rectification (Section 2; Exp 1-2);
(ii) a benchmark-audit methodology that found a bijective key-position shortcut in our own legacy benchmark and replaced it with a non-bijective content-addressable protocol (Exp 1 vs. Exp 2);
(iii) a residual-preservation protocol under pure cross-entropy that quantifies hallucination rates and row mass against simplex normalizers and virtual-sink softmax on equal footing (Exp 5);
(iv) fused Triton kernels with zero `exp()` calls, plus measurement forensics: an instrumented re-measurement that reproduced and falsified our own 6-layer end-to-end latency saving as a block-order artifact (drift-controlled re-measurement).

Supporting measurements (every table and figure regenerates from `results/*.json`):
1. **Single-Layer Induction via Causal FIR Key Binding & Benchmark Audit (Exp 1 vs. Exp 2):** By auditing attention weights at key vs. value positions, we uncover two shortcuts in the legacy fixed-position benchmark (Exp 1, where `KEY` is always at $t=0$ with a 1-to-1 bijection to `VAL` at $t=1$): left-padded causal FIR acts as a positional boundary detector at $t=0$ ($W_{\text{query}, 0} = 0.984$ for `sm_fir`, $0.554$ of $0.570$ total mass for `pn_fir_tau`), and random decoy injection overwrites competing keys in the filler. Designing a strict **non-bijective, random-position content-addressable recall task** (Exp 2, $M=3$ key-value pairs in $T=48$) eliminates both shortcuts: unfiltered 1-layer `sm` ($44.5\% \pm 0.9\%$) and `pn` ($35.8\% \pm 1.0\%$) fail completely, whereas causal FIR on $K$ physically shifts attention onto the **value slot** $j^*+1$ ($W_{\text{query}, j^*+1} = 0.792 \pm 0.011$ for `sm_fir` and $0.729 \pm 0.014$ for `pn_fir_tau`), achieving **$100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$** clean recall and $93.1\% \pm 1.9\%$ single-decoy recall at $\epsilon=1.0$, while degrading under heavy multi-decoy flooding at $n_d=8$ ($\epsilon=0.2$) to $56.2\%$ (`sm_fir`), $56.9\%$ (`pn_fir_tau`), and $38.8\%$ (`pn_fir_tau_reg`).
2. **Threshold Calibration & Post-Warmup Regularization (Exp 2 & Exp 3):** Across a 31-configuration sweep of $(\tau_0, \gamma, \text{silence\_mode})$, `pn_fir_tau` with peak gating ($\tau_0=0.0, \gamma \in [0.1, 0.5]$) achieves $100.0\%$ recall while suppressing noise row mass to $0.000\text{--}0.003$ on well-calibrated seeds (`gap = +0.432` to `+0.572`), compared to `gap = 0.000` for `sm_fir` and `+0.001` to `+0.021` for `pn_fir_c`. When trained exclusively on positive-match sequences where every query has a valid key in context, $\tau$ is under-constrained against background noise across random seeds ($\sum W_{\text{noise}} = 0.375 \pm 0.265$ in Exp 1; $0.218 \pm 0.167$ in Exp 2); introducing a lightweight post-warmup absent-key silence penalty (`pn_fir_tau_reg`) stabilizes $\tau = +0.115 \pm 0.021$ and drives both noise and absent-key attention to **exact zero across all seeds** ($\sum W_{\text{noise}} = 0.000 \pm 0.000$, `gap = +0.684 ± 0.030`).
3. **Simplex vs. Sub-Simplex Normalizers on Unanswerable Queries Under Pure Cross-Entropy Loss (Exp 5):** On a topic-coherent document task ($T=48$, $M=3$ pairs, 50/50 answerable vs. unanswerable queries) modeling RAG over documents that may not contain the queried fact—trained with **100% pure cross-entropy loss and zero auxiliary attention regularizer**—all four simplex-projected normalizers (`sm_fir`, `sparsemax_fir` $\alpha=2.0$, `entmax15_fir` $\alpha=1.5$, and `entmax_p10_fir` $\alpha=1.1, p=10$) fail residual preservation and hallucinate document topic values on **$27.2\%\text{--}34.4\%$** of unanswerable queries (`overall = 79.2%–84.0%`, $\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 1.000$). In contrast, sub-simplex rectified power-normalization (`pn_fir_tau` and `pn_fir`) achieves **$99.5\% \pm 0.4\%$** and **$99.7\% \pm 0.5\%$ overall accuracy** ($99.0\% \pm 0.7\%$ and $99.4\% \pm 0.9\%$ override, **$100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ residual preservation / $0.0\%$ hallucination rate**) and suppresses absent-key row mass to **$\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$** (`gap = +0.742 ± 0.034` and `+0.745 ± 0.035`). Because 50% unanswerable supervision under Cross-Entropy loss directly trains $W_Q, W_K$ to place unanswerable raw scores below zero ($-0.175 \pm 0.070$) and answerable peaks above zero ($+1.548 \pm 0.281$), $\tau$ stays near zero ($\tau = +0.011 \pm 0.020$) and fixed $\tau=0$ `pn_fir` performs equally well here—unlike positive-only training (Exp 1/Exp 2) where learnable $\tau$ is required to prevent row-death. Compared to virtual-sink Softmax (`softmax1_fir` and `softmax_sink_fir`), which achieves slightly higher discrete override accuracy ($100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ vs. $99.0\% \pm 0.7\%$) and higher active mass ($\sum W_{\text{ans}} = 0.863\text{--}0.864$ vs. $0.744$), `pn_fir_tau` achieves **$50\times$ lower continuous residual-stream contamination** on unanswerable queries ($\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$ vs. $0.100 \pm 0.005$ from $\exp(S_{t,j}) > 0$ tails).
4. **WikiText-2 Language Modeling & Layer-Wise $\tau_\ell$ Specialization (Exp 4):** In a 6-layer causal Transformer ($1,797,632$ base parameters) on word-level WikiText-2, `pn_fir_tau` adds only $+6$ scalar parameters ($1,797,638$ total). Under strictly matched same-process `BF16` training (2,400 steps, single seed), `softmax` (`Val PPL = 547.33`, `Test PPL = 515.55`), `sm_fir` (`547.29` / `515.18`), eager `pn_fir_tau` (`546.75` / `515.56`), row-fused `pn_fir_tau_triton` (`545.78` / `515.40`), and Flash-tiled `pn_fir_tau_flash` (`546.78` / `516.11`) differ by at most $0.3\%$ from one another (single seed per variant; no variance estimate is available), and across a precision-controlled 50,000-step comparison (`results/exp4d_softmax_50k_bf16.json` seed 42 plus `results/exp4e_multiseed_50k.json` seeds 43–44: the historical `FP32` softmax 50k was re-run under the identical `BF16+TF32` schedule, FP32 first 1k steps then resume) `pn_fir_tau` reaches **`Val PPL = 122.15 \pm 0.72` / `Test PPL = 114.80 \pm 0.49`** (vs. `124.37 \pm 0.26` / `117.13 \pm 0.04` for matched-precision `softmax`: $-2.22 \pm 0.46$ val / $-2.33 \pm 0.49$ test PPL, 3 seeds per variant, win on all 3 seeds) with zero causal leakage (`max_past_delta = 0.0`) and self-organized negative layer thresholds $\tau_\ell \in [-0.282, -0.064]$.
5. **Fused Triton Kernels & Hardware Acceleration (`PnFirTauTritonMHA` & `PnFirTauFlashMHA`):** While unfused eager PyTorch `pn_fir_tau` incurs $+68.3\%$ (`BF16`, $164.4\text{ ms}$ vs. $97.7\text{ ms}$ for `softmax` and $99.2\text{ ms}$ for `sm_fir`) to $+73.3\%$ (`FP32`, $244.2\text{ ms}$ vs. $140.9\text{ ms}$) full-LM step-time overhead and $+18.5\%$ VRAM ($6.11\text{ GB}$ vs. $5.16\text{ GB}$) due to materializing multiple $[B, H, N, N]$ intermediate tensors, our custom fused Triton kernels ([`src/triton_pnfirtau.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/triton_pnfirtau.py)) eliminate this bottleneck with high `FP32` numerical fidelity (`out_max_diff = 2.38e-07`, rel `4.38e-07`; `dx_max_diff = 1.16e-06`–`1.32e-06`, rel `5.36e-07`–`6.08e-07`; `dtau_diff = 3.91e-05`–`4.01e-05`, rel `5.56e-06`–`5.70e-06` across a $264{,}192$-element reduction). Because $(S'_{t,j})_+^{10}$ is evaluated in 3 register multiplications with **zero transcendental `exp()` calls** and requires no online max-rescaling across tiles, row-fused **`pn_fir_tau_triton`** cuts full-LM `BF16` step time to **$75.7\text{ ms}$** (**$2.17\times$ faster than eager and $22.5\%$ faster than `softmax`** at $4.97\text{ GB}$ VRAM), while IO-aware Flash-tiled **`pn_fir_tau_flash`** cuts full-LM `BF16` step time to **$66.8\text{ ms}$** (**$2.46\times$ faster than eager and $31.6\%$ faster than `softmax`** at $4.52\text{ GB}$ VRAM). On an isolated 6-layer MHA stack at fixed batch size $B=64$, `pn_fir_tau_flash` scales monotonically across $N \in \{256, 512, 1024\}$ (**$17.66 \pm 2.28\text{ ms} \to 20.60 \pm 2.73\text{ ms} \to 46.77 \pm 0.54\text{ ms}$**, $109.3\text{ MB} \to 201.8\text{ MB} \to 386.8\text{ MB}$ while Eager baselines OOM $>12\text{ GB}$ at $N=1024, B=64$), and at $N=1024, B=16$ achieves **$21.70\times$ speedup** ($18.13 \pm 2.44\text{ ms}$ vs. $393.42 \pm 0.43\text{ ms}$) and **$66.5\times$ VRAM reduction** ($109.3\text{ MB}$ vs. $7,268.3\text{ MB}$).
6. **Token-Shift Ablation: How Much Memory Robustness Needs (Exp 6):** Replacing the 7-tap FIR with a 2-tap RWKV-style shift on $K$ (fixed $[0.5, 0.5]$ or learnable per-channel $\mu$, same position) shows binding needs only lag-1 memory — both shift variants reach $100.0\%$ clean recall on both decoy protocols — while multi-decoy flooding favours *shorter* memory: at $n_d=8$, `sm_shiftmu` holds **$99.9\% \pm 0.2\%$** against $56.2\% \pm 7.2\%$ for 7-tap `sm_fir` ($W_{\text{val}} = 0.970$ vs. $0.792$). The rectified branch pays for short memory with seed instability (`pn_shiftmu_tau` clean $76.5\% \pm 10.9\%$ on Exp 1, $92.2\% \pm 11.0\%$ on Exp 2 with $\tau$–$\mu$ equilibria varying per seed), so the 7-tap filter is the stable partner of a single scalar $\tau$.
7. **Width Envelope and the Tau Fixed Point (Exp 4f-A/4g):** The pathology amplifies with width — uncalibrated raw magnitudes diverge (tens to hundreds) rather than drift, and a linear drift-cancellation law is rejected ($R^2 = 0.45$). The calibrated fixed point is width-invariant ($\tau^* \in [-1.4, -0.7]$ wherever convergence happens); only its basin from $\tau_0 = 0$ collapses outside $d \in [32, 64]$. Negative initialization ($\tau_0 = -1.02$) rescues d=128 on all seeds — a basin problem, not a representational wall.

**Scope.** This is a controlled mechanistic study of how causal FIR smoothing interacts with sub-simplex normalization; the 1.8M-parameter language model and the synthetic recall tasks are deliberately small sandboxes that isolate the mechanism, not evidence of LLM-scale behaviour. We frame the contribution as a calibrated diagnostic and tool, and treat scale-up as future work.

## 2. Mathematical Formulation and the Score-Shift Pathology

Let $X \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times D}$ be the input sequence representation with model dimension $D$, $H$ attention heads, and per-head dimension $d_k = D / H$. The linear projections are $Q = X W_Q$, $K = X W_K$, and $V = X W_V$, reshaped to $\mathbb{R}^{B \times H \times T \times d_k}$. Everything described below is implemented verbatim in [`src/pnfirtau_attention.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/pnfirtau_attention.py).

### 2.1 Causal FIR Low-Pass Filtering Strictly on the Key Stream $K$

Non-causal frequency-domain filtering (`fft_lowpass_noncausal` in `sm_fft`, `pn_fft`, `qe_pn_fft`) multiplies the full-sequence discrete Fourier transform by a Gaussian spectral mask $\exp(-\frac{1}{2}(f / (T/\sigma_{\text{div}}))^2)$. Because a symmetric frequency response corresponds to a two-sided impulse response in time, modifying token $t$ alters filtered keys at past positions $t' < t$. Our causal diagnostic confirms a massive violation of autoregressive causality (`past_key_leak = 0.785`).

To enforce strict autoregressive causality (`past_key_leak = 0.0`), we apply a causal Finite Impulse Response (FIR) depthwise 1D convolution of length $K_f = 7$ (`causal_fir` and `causal_gaussian_taps` in [`src/pnfirtau_attention.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/pnfirtau_attention.py#L46-L81)) **exclusively to the key stream $K$**, using half-Gaussian weights with $\sigma = \max((K_f - 1)/3.0,\, 0.5) = (7 - 1)/3.0 = 2.0$:
$$h_m = \frac{\exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}(m / \sigma)^2\right)}{\sum_{r=0}^{K_f-1} \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}(r / \sigma)^2\right)}, \quad m \in \{0, 1, \dots, 6\}.$$
Numerically, for $K_f = 7$ and $\sigma = 2.0$, the exact 7-tap impulse response is:
$$h = [0.3329,\; 0.2938,\; 0.2019,\; 0.1081,\; 0.0451,\; 0.0146,\; 0.0037], \quad \sum_{m=0}^{6} h_m = 1.0000, \quad \|h\|_2^2 = \sum_{m=0}^{6} h_m^2 = 0.2518.$$
Causal left-padding by $K_f - 1 = 6$ zeros ensures that the filtered key trajectory $\tilde{K}$ depends exclusively on current and past tokens ($m \ge 0$), while **both the query stream $Q$ and the value stream $V$ remain completely unfiltered** (Figure 2):
$$\tilde{K}_{b,h,t,d} = \sum_{m=0}^{\min(t,\, K_f-1)} h_m\, K_{b,h,t-m,d}, \qquad S_{b,h,t,j} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{d_k}} \langle Q_{b,h,t},\, \tilde{K}_{b,h,j} \rangle \quad (j \le t).$$
Filtering strictly $K$ (leaving $Q$ and $V$ sharp) has a direct mechanistic justification: at query time $t$, the query $Q_t$ represents the instantaneous key $k^*$ being sought right now (which should not be blurred with preceding filler tokens), whereas at context position $j^*+1$ (where the value $v^*$ is stored in $V_{j^*+1}$), the filtered key $\tilde{K}_{j^*+1} = h_0 K_{j^*+1} + h_1 K_{j^*} + \dots$ blends the preceding key identity $K_{j^*} = W_K E(k^*)$ onto the value slot $j^*+1$ (Figure 1).

### 2.2 Proposition 1: Incompatibility of Translation-Invariance and Baseline-Shift Selective Silence

Why does anchoring selective silence against uniform baseline score shifts require a threshold $\tau$ (or a virtual sink column) once we move from Softmax/Entmax to a sub-simplex normalizer? We formalize this via an exact invariance statement along uniform shift rays $s - c\mathbf{1}$.

> **Proposition 1 (Invariance of Row Mass Along Uniform Shift Rays).** *Let $\Phi: \mathbb{R}^{n} \to [0, 1]^n$ be any attention row normalizer satisfying sub-simplex validity $\sum_{j=1}^n \Phi_j(s) \le 1$ for all $s \in \mathbb{R}^n$. Suppose $\Phi$ is translation-invariant:*
> $$\Phi(s + c\mathbf{1}) = \Phi(s) \quad \forall s \in \mathbb{R}^n,\; \forall c \in \mathbb{R}.$$
> *Then total row mass $M(s) = \sum_{j=1}^n \Phi_j(s)$ is constant along every uniform shift ray $\{s - c\mathbf{1} : c \in \mathbb{R}\}$: $M(s - c\mathbf{1}) = M(s + c\mathbf{1}) = M(s)$. In particular, $\Phi$ cannot transition from active retrieval ($M(s) \approx 1$) to selective silence ($M(s - c\mathbf{1}) \to 0$ as $c \to +\infty$) under a uniform downward shift of the baseline score distribution.*

*Proof.* Immediate from $\sum_{j=1}^n \Phi_j(s - c\mathbf{1}) = \sum_{j=1}^n \Phi_j(s)$ for all $c \in \mathbb{R}$. Any simplex-projected normalizer (Softmax, Sparsemax, $\alpha$-Entmax) satisfies $M(s) \equiv 1$ everywhere on $\mathbb{R}^n$. Conversely, any sub-simplex normalizer whose row mass responds to uniform baseline shifts ($M(s - c\mathbf{1}) \to 0$ as $c \to +\infty$) must break translation-invariance by anchoring scores against an external threshold $\tau$; if a sub-simplex operator instead restores translation-invariance by subtracting the row mean $\mu(s)$ (as in `pn_fir_c`), its row mass becomes a function solely of **within-row score dispersion** $s - \mu(s)\mathbf{1}$ rather than absolute score level. $\square$

### 2.3 Why Causal FIR Key Smoothing Creates a Negative Score Shift—and Why `sm_fir` Is Immune

When $\tilde{K}_j$ is formed by a convex combination of token keys with $\ell_2$ energy $\|h\|_2^2 = 0.2518 < 1$, two phenomena occur during gradient-based training:
1. **Key Variance Contraction:** For uncorrelated background tokens, the variance of $\tilde{K}_j$ contracts by $\|h\|_2^2 \approx 0.2518$, compressing the dynamic range of dot products $S_{t,j} = \langle Q_t, \tilde{K}_j \rangle / \sqrt{d_k}$.
2. **Negative Background Drift:** To separate the target key-value pair from background filler tokens under cross-entropy loss, the projection matrices $W_Q, W_K$ learn embeddings whose pairwise inner products with filler tokens are negative ($\mathbb{E}[S_{t,j}] < 0$). Because causal FIR averages each key with up to 6 preceding filler tokens, even the peak target score $S_{t, j^*+1}$ is pulled downward by the negative background terms in the 7-tap window.

By Proposition 1, the two normalization families react in opposite ways to this downward score shift:
- **Softmax (`sm_fir`), Sparsemax, and Entmax are shift-invariant:** Because $\operatorname{softmax}(S_t - c) = \operatorname{softmax}(S_t)$, `sm_fir` depends *only* on score differences $S_{t,j} - S_{t,j'}$. In Experiment 1, our score diagnostics reveal that in trained `sm_fir`, **$100.0\%$ of query rows have strictly negative scores** (`raw_frac_pos = 0.0%`, `raw_rows_all_neg = 100.0%`, with mean row maximum $\max_j S_{t,j} = -0.37$ and row mean $-1.73$). Yet because the target slot still achieves the *least negative* score in the row, `sm_fir` achieves **$100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$** accuracy!
- **Uncalibrated Rectified Power-Normalization (`pn_fir`, fixed $\tau=0$) suffers dead-row starvation under positive-only training:** Power-normalization applies a hard ReLU threshold at zero before raising scores to the $p$-th power ($\varepsilon = 10^{-12}$ in [`src/pnfirtau_attention.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/pnfirtau_attention.py#L107)):
  $$S^+_{t,j} = \max(S_{t,j},\, 0), \qquad \hat{w}_{t,j} = \frac{(S^+_{t,j})^p}{\sum_{m=0}^{t} (S^+_{t,m})^p + \varepsilon}.$$
  Whenever an entire query row drifts below zero ($\max_{j \le t} S_{t,j} \le 0$), every entry is clipped by ReLU: $S^+_{t,j} = 0\;\forall j$, producing $\hat{w}_{t,j} = 0$ and **exact zero gradients** ($\partial \mathcal{L} / \partial S_{t,j} = 0$). In Experiment 1 (where every training sequence is a positive retrieval query), uncalibrated `pn_fir` has only $8.7\% \pm 8.6\%$ positive raw scores (seed range $2.5\%$–$20.8\%$) and **$44.5\% \pm 23.0\%$ of all query rows are completely dead** (`raw_rows_all_neg` = $44.5\% \pm 23.0\%$; seed range $12.1\%$–$62.0\%$), causing training to stall at $64.4\% \pm 6.8\%$ clean accuracy ($61.4\%, 58.1\%, 73.9\%$ across seeds) (Figure 3).

![Figure 3: Diagram of the score-shift pathology. Left: smoothing lowers the whole score distribution, which a translation-invariant normaliser cancels but a rectified one does not. Right: the four regimes and why a negative shift is invisible to Softmax yet removes mass under a fixed threshold. Source: `paper_en.tex` (Proposition 1) and `src/pnfirtau_attention.py`.](figures/fig16_schematic_score_shift_mechanism.png)

The parameters $p=10$ and $\sigma=2.0$ are fixed a priori; a sensitivity sweep over these values is left for future work.

### 2.4 Why Row-Mean Centering (`pn_fir_c`) Substitutes Dispersion for Thresholding and Leaks Noise

An intuitive attempt to rescue `pn_fir` is causal row-mean centering (`pn_fir_c`), which subtracts the causal row mean $\mu_t = \frac{1}{t+1} \sum_{j=0}^{t} S_{t,j}$ from every valid entry prior to ReLU:
$$S^{\text{center}}_{t,j} = S_{t,j} - \frac{1}{t+1} \sum_{m=0}^{t} S_{t,m} \quad (j \le t).$$
Subtracting $\mu_t$ makes `pn_fir_c` **invariant to uniform baseline shifts** ($S^{\text{center}}(S_t + c\mathbf{1}) = S^{\text{center}}(S_t)$), eliminating dead rows (`cal_rows_all_neg = 0.0%`) and restoring $100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ recall on Exp 1 ($96.5\% \pm 4.9\%$ on Exp 2). However, in any non-constant row of scores, subtracting the sample mean guarantees that roughly half of the entries become strictly positive ($44.4\%$ of entries in Exp 1) with positive peak $\max_{j \le t} S^{\text{center}}_{t,j} = \max_{j \le t} S_{t,j} - \mu_t > 0$. This has three direct consequences across our experimental regimes:
1. **Without a silence gate ($\gamma = \text{None}$, as in Exp 2 and `pn_fir_c_ungated` in Exp 1):** Because $\max_{j \le t} S^{\text{center}}_{t,j} > 0$ on every non-constant row, the power-normalized weights sum to $\sum_j W_{t,j} = 1.000 \pm 0.000$ on both matching and pure-noise rows (`Silence Gap = 0.000`).
2. **With a peak gate ($\gamma = 0.5$) under positive-only training (Exp 1 & Exp 3):** The gate $g_t = \frac{\max_j S^{\text{center}}_{t,j}}{\max_j S^{\text{center}}_{t,j} + \gamma}$ depends solely on **within-row dispersion** $\max_j S_{t,j} - \mu_t$. When evaluated on uncalibrated random noise inputs $x_{\text{noise}} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, the dot-product dispersion across $T=15$ positions is large relative to $\gamma$, driving $\sum_j W_j(\text{noise}) = 0.938 \pm 0.007$ (`Silence Gap = +0.017 ± 0.004`).
3. **With a peak gate ($\gamma = 0.5$) under end-to-end unanswerable-query training (Exp 5):** When 50% of training queries are unanswerable and the loss penalizes residual-stream corruption, the projection matrix $W_Q$ can partially reduce $g_t$ on unanswerable queries by shrinking the query norm $\|Q_t\|_2$ (which scales within-row dispersion $\max_j S_{t,j} - \mu_t \propto \|Q_t\|_2$ toward zero), achieving a partial silence gap of $+0.451 \pm 0.032$ ($\sum W_{\text{ans}} = 0.716 \pm 0.018$ vs. $\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.265 \pm 0.045$). Yet because $\max_j S^{\text{center}}_{t,j} > 0$ cannot be thresholded to zero without collapsing $\|Q_t\|_2 \to 0$, `pn_fir_c` still leaks **$\approx 124\times$ more unanswerable attention mass** than `pn_fir_tau` ($0.265$ vs. $0.002$) while suffering severe answerable override degradation ($88.5\% \pm 8.9\%$ vs. $99.0\% \pm 0.7\%$).

### 2.5 The `pn_fir_tau` Architecture: Learnable Threshold $\tau$ and Peak Silence Gate

To calibrate the global score distribution without restoring within-row shift-invariance, **`pn_fir_tau`** introduces a **single learnable scalar parameter $\tau \in \mathbb{R}$ per attention layer** (initialized at $\tau_0 = 0.0$), shared across heads and sequence positions (`power_norm_peak_gate` in [`src/pnfirtau_attention.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/pnfirtau_attention.py#L84-L119)):
1. **Learnable Threshold Calibration & ReLU Rectification:**
   $$S'_{t,j} = (S_{t,j} - \tau) \cdot \mathbb{I}(j \le t), \qquad S^+_{t,j} = \max(S'_{t,j},\, 0).$$
2. **Power-Law Sharpening ($p = 10.0$, $\varepsilon = 10^{-12}$):**
   $$\hat{w}_{t,j} = \frac{(S^+_{t,j})^p}{\sum_{m=0}^{t} (S^+_{t,m})^p + \varepsilon}.$$
3. **Peak-Driven Selective Silence Gate ($\gamma = 0.5$):**
   Let $M_t = \max_{0 \le m \le t} S^+_{t,m}$ be the maximum rectified calibrated score in row $t$. The row-level silence gate $g_t \in [0, 1)$ is:
   $$g_t = \frac{M_t}{M_t + \gamma}, \qquad W_{t,j} = g_t \cdot \hat{w}_{t,j}, \qquad Y_t = \sum_{j=0}^{t} W_{t,j} V_j.$$
   Total row attention mass is $\sum_{j=0}^{t} W_{t,j} = g_t$ whenever $M_t > 0$, and $0$ when $M_t = 0$. Unlike `Entmax` (where $\tau^*(s_t)$ is a data-dependent Lagrange multiplier enforcing $\sum_j W_{t,j} \equiv 1$ on every row), $\tau$ in `pn_fir_tau` is a **context-independent learned threshold**: rows whose peak score $M_t$ falls near or below $\tau$ are silenced ($g_t \to 0$), while rows with a true match ($M_t \gg \gamma$) pass through ($g_t \to 1$).

### 2.6 IO-Aware Fused Triton Kernels (`PnFirTauTritonMHA` & `PnFirTauFlashMHA`)

In eager PyTorch (`PnFirTauMHA`), `SoftmaxMHA` and `SmFirMHA` dispatch `F.softmax` to a single fused CUDA kernel (`aten::_safe_softmax`), whereas `power_norm_peak_gate` executes a 6-op Python chain (`* scale`, `- score_tau`, `masked_fill`, `F.relu`, `.pow(10)`, `.sum`/`.amax`/gate division) that materializes four $[B, H, N, N]$ intermediate tensors in GPU High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM). However, once fused in SRAM registers ([`src/triton_pnfirtau.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/triton_pnfirtau.py)), `pn_fir_tau` possesses three fundamental hardware advantages over `Softmax`:
1. **Zero Transcendentals ($u^{10}$ in 3 Register Multiplications):** Evaluating $\exp(s_j - m_t)$ requires Special Function Unit (SFU) transcendental instructions on every score entry. For $p = 10$, rectified power-normalization computes $u_{t,j} = \max(S'_{t,j}, 0)$ and raises it to the 10th power in **3 register multiplications** ($u^2 = u \cdot u$, $u^4 = u^2 \cdot u^2$, $u^{10} = (u^4 \cdot u^4) \cdot u^2$; and $u^9 = (u^4 \cdot u^4) \cdot u$ in backward) with **zero `exp()` calls**.
2. **Rescaling-Free Online Tiling:** Standard FlashAttention [10] must rescale the running output accumulator $\tilde{O}_t$ and denominator by $\exp(m_{\text{old}} - m_{\text{new}})$ whenever a new key-value tile increases the running row maximum. Because $P_{t,j} = (S'_{t,j})_+^{10}$ is anchored at the fixed threshold $\tau$ rather than the row maximum, the unnormalized output accumulator $\tilde{O}_t = \sum_{\text{tiles}} P_{\text{tile}} V_{\text{tile}}$, power sum $Z_t = \sum_j P_{t,j} + \varepsilon$, and row peak $M_t = \max_j (S'_{t,j})_+$ accumulate **linearly across tiles with zero intermediate rescaling**, normalizing only once at row completion: $O_t = (g_t / Z_t) \tilde{O}_t$.
3. **Single-Pass Analytical Backward & Tile-Level Early-Exit Branches:** For any active row ($M_t > 0$), since $O_t = g_t \sum_{j=0}^t \hat{w}_{t,j} V_j$ and $dW_{t,j} = \langle dO_t, V_j \rangle$, the inner product between earlier-layer weight gradients and normalized weights satisfies the exact identity:
   $$D_t \triangleq \sum_{j=0}^{t} dW_{t,j}\, \hat{w}_{t,j} = \frac{\langle dO_t,\, O_t \rangle}{g_t}, \qquad dS'_{t,j} = \mathbb{I}(u_{t,j} > 0)\!\left[\frac{p\, g_t}{Z_t} u_{t,j}^{p-1}\big(dW_{t,j} - D_t\big) + \frac{\mathbb{I}(u_{t,j} = M_t)}{N_{\max,t}} D_t \frac{\gamma}{(M_t + \gamma)^2}\right],$$
   where $N_{\max,t} = \sum_j \mathbb{I}(u_{t,j} = M_t)$ matches PyTorch's `amax` subgradient tie-breaking (`GradTau` accumulates $-\sum_{t,j} dS'_{t,j}$ per KV block into a deterministic buffer summed in `FP64`). Furthermore, whenever an entire $64 \times 64$ $[B_M \times B_N]$ tile satisfies $\max u_{t,j} \le 0$, hardware conditional branches (`if tile_max > 0.0` in forward; `if tl.max(peak) > 0.0` and `if tl.max(u) > 0.0` in backward) skip HBM loads of $V, Q, O, dO$ and `tl.dot` matrix multiplications for that tile (which we profile in Section 3.5 both at the isolated kernel level and in the full 6-layer MHA stack).

We provide three custom Triton kernel suites in [`src/triton_pnfirtau.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/triton_pnfirtau.py), exposed via two drop-in MHA classes in [`src/pnfirtau_attention.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/pnfirtau_attention.py#L250-L317):
- **`triton_causal_fir`:** Fused zero-copy depthwise 1D causal FIR kernel ($K_f=7$) operating directly on strided `qkv` slices without `.transpose().contiguous()` or `F.pad` allocations.
- **`PnFirTauTritonMHA` (`triton_pn_fir_tau_scores`):** Row-fused Triton kernel (the direct $O(N^2)$-materialized counterpart to `aten::_safe_softmax`, supporting elementwise attention dropout and `return_weights=True`).
- **`PnFirTauFlashMHA` (`flash_pn_fir_tau_attention`):** FlashAttention-2 style IO-aware online-tiled Triton kernel with $O(N)$ memory complexity ($B_M = B_N = 64$, saving only three $[B, H, N]$ vectors $M_t, Z_t, N_{\max,t}$ for backward) and single-pass KV-parallel backward (5 `tl.dot` calls per active tile).

#### Deep Algorithmic Audit of Experiment 1: Uncovering Two Hidden Benchmark Shortcuts

While Table 1 confirms the score-shift pathology (`pn_fir` has $44.5\% \pm 23.0\%$ dead rows and $64.4\%$ clean recall, whereas `pn_fir_tau` learns $\tau = -1.010 \pm 0.083$, reduces dead rows to $6.9\%$, and achieves $100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ recall), our diagnostic instrumentation (`w_key_pos` at $t=0$ vs. `w_val_pos` at $t=1$) reveals two critical anomalies in the legacy task design:
1. **Why do `sm_fir` ($W_{t,0} = 0.984, W_{t,1} = 0.013$) and `pn_fir_tau` ($W_{t,0} = 0.554, W_{t,1} = 0.012$) attend to `KEY` ($t=0$) instead of `VAL` ($t=1$)?** Because the legacy task uses a **fixed 1-to-1 bijection** (`VAL = KEY + 5`) and **always places `KEY` at the sequence boundary $t=0$**, the model does not need to read the `VAL` token at $t=1$ at all! If the attention head simply attends to $t=0$, its value projection $W_V E(\text{KEY})$ can linearly map $\text{KEY} \mapsto \text{KEY}+5$. Worse still, because causal FIR left-pads the key sequence with $K_f-1 = 6$ zeros, position $t=0$ is the *only* position in the sequence whose filtered key $\tilde{K}_0 = h_0 K_0$ is uncontaminated by preceding tokens. Thus, on Exp 1, causal FIR acts as a **positional boundary detector for $t=0$** rather than a content-addressable key-to-value bridge!
2. **Why does unfiltered `sm` jump from $71.5\%$ on clean sequences to $91.7\%$ at `d@0.05` and $100.0\%$ at `nd=8`?** In the legacy generator, filler tokens at positions $2..13$ are drawn uniformly from $\{1..23\}$, which *includes the competing keys $\{1..5\}$*. On clean sequences, random competing keys appear in the filler and confuse `sm` ($71.5\%$). When the legacy decoy test overwrites up to $n_d=8$ filler positions with $(1-\epsilon) E(\text{KEY}) + \epsilon E(\text{NOISE})$, it overwrites competing false keys with copies of the *true* key, which—under the bijection $\text{VAL} = \text{KEY}+5$—all map to the exact same target label!

Reporting Experiment 1 without exposing these two shortcuts would be scientifically misleading. We therefore designed Experiment 2 to eliminate both shortcuts completely.

### 3.2 Experiment 2: True Content-Addressable Non-Bijective Recall & Distractor Decoys

In Experiment 2 (`exp2_content_addressable_decoy.py` $\to$ [`results/exp2_content_addressable_decoy.json`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/results/exp2_content_addressable_decoy.json)), we construct a strict content-addressable associative recall benchmark ($T=48$, $V=24$, $D=32$, 1 head, 2,500 epochs):
- **Zero Bijection ($M=3$ Random Key-Value Pairs):** Each sequence contains $M=3$ distinct keys drawn from $K_{\text{vocab}} = \{1..8\}$, each paired with an *independently* permuted value drawn from $V_{\text{vocab}} = \{9..16\}$. Knowing $k^*$ gives zero information about $v^*$ without reading the token at $j^*+1$.
- **Random Interior Positions (Zero Boundary Shortcut):** The $M=3$ pairs $(k_m, v_m)$ are placed at random non-overlapping interior positions $j_m \in [2, 38]$, and all filler positions are drawn strictly from $F_{\text{vocab}} = \{17..23\}$ (disjoint from keys and values). Position $T-1=47$ holds the query $k^* = k_{m^*}$.
- **True Distractor Decoys:** At evaluation, $n_d \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$ isolated filler positions (separated by $\ge 2$ slots from all true pairs) are overwritten with $(1-\epsilon) E(k^*) + \epsilon E(\text{filler})$. Because the token following a decoy is a random filler token rather than $v^*$, attending to a decoy (or to the slot after a decoy) actively destroys retrieval accuracy.
- **Post-Warmup Absent-Key Silence Regularization (`pn_fir_tau_reg`):** When `pn_fir_tau` is trained exclusively on positive-match sequences, $\tau$ is calibrated to pass the target peak $S_{t, j^*+1}$, but on some random seeds $\tau$ settles slightly below the upper tail of pure noise rows ($\tau = -0.256 \pm 0.082$, giving $\sum W_{\text{noise}} = 0.218 \pm 0.167$ across seeds: $0.202, 0.022, 0.430$). In `pn_fir_tau_reg`, after a 1,000-epoch warmup, we auxiliary-sample 32 absent-key sequences per step (where $k^*$ is not among the 3 context keys) and add a lightweight silence penalty $\lambda_{\text{sil}} \cdot \mathbb{E}[\sum_j W_{\text{query}, j}]$ ($\lambda_{\text{sil}} = 0.05$).

Table 2, Figure 4 and Figure 5 establish four findings:
1. **Direct Proof of Causal FIR Value-Slot Binding:** Once the bijective boundary shortcut is removed, unfiltered `sm` ($44.5\% \pm 0.9\%$) and `pn` ($35.8\% \pm 1.0\%$) fail completely: because $M=3$ values are present in the context, the best an unfiltered 1-layer head can do is attend equally to all 3 value tokens ($W_{t, j^*+1} \approx 0.234$, yielding $\sim 1/3$ random guessing among the 3 context values). Adding the causal FIR filter on $K$ shifts attention weight directly onto the **target value slot $j^*+1$**: $W_{t, j^*+1} = 0.792 \pm 0.011$ (vs. $W_{t, j^*} = 0.132$) for `sm_fir`, $W_{t, j^*+1} = 0.729 \pm 0.014$ (vs. $W_{t, j^*} = 0.063$) for `pn_fir_tau`, and $W_{t, j^*+1} = 0.647 \pm 0.007$ (vs. $W_{t, j^*} = 0.034$) for `pn_fir_tau_reg` (with $\text{argmax}_j W_{t,j} = j^*+1$ on $98.0\%\text{--}100.0\%$ of test queries!).
2. **Realistic Multi-Decoy Behavior and Linear Superposition Bound:** Unlike Exp 1 where decoys artificially helped by overwriting competing keys, true distractor decoys in Exp 2 cause a realistic, monotonic accuracy decline as the context is flooded with $n_d = 1 \to 8$ copies of $k^*$: `pn_fir_tau` holds $95.6\%$ at $n_d=1$ and $90.5\%$ at $n_d=2$, declining to $74.7\%$ ($n_d=4$) and $56.9\%$ ($n_d=8$) (`56.2%` for `sm_fir`, `38.8%` for `pn_fir_tau_reg`) because each decoy injects $h_1 K(k^*)$ onto the filler slot immediately following the decoy. Why does a single decoy ($n_d=1, \epsilon=0.2$) only reduce `pn_fir_tau` from $100.0\%$ to $95.8\%$ instead of $50\%$? Because the model learns to give value tokens in $V_{\text{vocab}}$ a small positive key bias relative to filler tokens in $F_{\text{vocab}}$, so the true pair score $\langle Q_{\text{query}}, h_0 K(v^*) + h_1 K(k^*) \rangle$ systematically exceeds the decoy-followed-by-filler score $\langle Q_{\text{query}}, h_0 K(\text{filler}) + h_1 K(k^*) \rangle$.
3. **Score Calibration Stability (`pn_fir` vs. `pn_fir_c` vs. `pn_fir_tau`):** Uncalibrated `pn_fir` fails to converge to $100\%$ on 2 of 3 seeds ($88.9\%, 77.4\%, 100.0\% \to 88.8\% \pm 9.2\%$), and `pn_fir_c` drops to $89.6\%$ on seed 1234 ($96.5\% \pm 4.9\%$) while exhibiting zero silence (`gap = 0.000`). Both `pn_fir_tau` and `pn_fir_tau_reg` achieve **$100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$** across all 3 seeds.
4. **Why Calibration Requires Absent-Key Signal (`pn_fir_tau` vs. `pn_fir_tau_reg`):** Without absent-key supervision during training, `pn_fir_tau` achieves a strong mean noise silence gap of $+0.608 \pm 0.135$ ($\sum W_{\text{match}} = 0.826$ vs. $\sum W_{\text{noise}} = 0.218$), but exhibits seed-to-seed variance on noise ($\sum W_{\text{noise}} \in \{0.202, 0.022, 0.430\}$) and remains active on absent-key queries ($\sum W_{\text{absent}} = 0.607$). Adding the post-warmup absent-key regularizer in `pn_fir_tau_reg` shifts the learned threshold from $\tau = -0.256 \pm 0.082$ to $\tau = +0.115 \pm 0.021$, driving **both noise and absent-key attention mass to $0.000 \pm 0.000$ across all 3 seeds** (`gap = +0.684 ± 0.030`). Moreover, in Section 3.4 (Experiment 5), we show that when unanswerable queries naturally occur in the task distribution, **pure cross-entropy loss alone** (with zero auxiliary regularizer) drives `pn_fir_tau` to $\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$.

Three design rules emerge from Table 5 and Figure 9 (right panel):
1. **Initialization Constraint ($\tau_0 \le 0$):** Initializing $\tau_0 = +0.5$ places the threshold above the initial Gaussian dot-product distribution ($\max_j S_{t,j} < +0.5$ at step 0). 2. **Why `peak` Gating Outperforms `mass_relu` Gating:** Under `mass_relu` ($g_t = \frac{\sum_j S^+_{t,j}}{\sum_j S^+_{t,j} + \gamma}$), small positive residuals across $T=15$ positions sum together ($\sum_j S^+_{t,j} \gg \gamma$), keeping the gate open on noise ($\sum W_{\text{noise}} = 0.815$ at $\gamma=0.5$, `gap = +0.010`). 3. **Why `pn_fir_c` Fails on Uncalibrated Noise Across $\gamma$:** Under positive-only training, varying $\gamma \in [\text{None}, 0.50]$ on `pn_fir_c` leaves $\sum W_{\text{noise}} \in [0.938, 1.000]$ and `gap` $\le +0.021$, because the dispersion $\max_j S_{t,j} - \mu_t$ of random Gaussian noise rows remains large relative to $\gamma$.

### 3.4 Experiment 5: Context Override vs. Residual Stream Preservation Against `Sparsemax`, `Entmax`, and `Softmax Sinks` Under Pure Cross-Entropy Loss

A critical question raised by our theoretical analysis is whether sub-simplex selective silence provides a concrete end-to-end task advantage over `sm_fir` and existing sparse normalizers (`Sparsemax` [4] and $\alpha$-`Entmax` [5, 6]) **under pure task Cross-Entropy loss with zero auxiliary attention regularization**.

In a multi-layer Transformer, each attention head reads from and writes additively to the residual stream:
$$y_{\text{query}} = v_{\text{residual}} + \alpha_{\text{ctx}} \sum_{j < \text{query}} W_{\text{query}, j}\, V_j.$$
When the queried key $k^*$ is present in the context (**Answerable / Context Override**), the attention head must retrieve the paired value $v^*$ from slot $j^*+1$ and override the prior residual state $v_{\text{residual}}$. When $k^*$ is *absent* from the context (**Unanswerable / Residual Preservation**), the attention head must abstain ($\sum_j W_{\text{query}, j} \to 0$) so that $v_{\text{residual}}$ is preserved intact! Moreover, in realistic topic-coherent documents (and when $V$ is tied to token class representations), every context token carries a non-zero semantic value $V_j$ belonging to the document's topic distribution $v_{\text{sub}}$, so no context token has $V_j = 0$ to act as a free "zero-value trash bin."

Table 6 and Figure 9 (left panel) demonstrate four decisive results:
1. **All Four Simplex-Projected Normalizers (`sm_fir`, `sparsemax_fir`, `entmax15_fir`, `entmax_p10_fir`) Fail Residual Preservation (`Hallucination Rate = 27.2%–34.4%`):** Because $\sum_j W_{\text{query}, j} \equiv 1$, none of these normalizers can abstain on unanswerable queries ($\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 1.000$). The only way a simplex-constrained head can attempt to preserve $v_{\text{default}}$ is by spreading its weight uniformly across the 3 document topic values so their unit vectors partially cancel out ($2.5 \times \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.833 < 1.0$), which forces it to also dilute weight on answerable queries ($W_{t, j^*+1} \in [0.453, 0.587]$) and still fails whenever random filler frequencies are unbalanced ($27.2\%$ hallucination rate for `sm_fir`). 2. **Sub-Simplex Power-Normalization (`pn_fir_tau` & `pn_fir`) Achieves $100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ Residual Preservation ($0.0\%$ Hallucination Rate) and $\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$ Under Pure Cross-Entropy Loss:** With zero auxiliary attention penalty, pure cross-entropy loss trains `pn_fir_tau` to place all unanswerable query scores strictly below $\tau = +0.011 \pm 0.020$ ($\max_j S^{\text{raw}}_{\text{unans}} = -0.175 \pm 0.070 < \tau$, while $\max_j S^{\text{raw}}_{\text{ans}} = +1.548 \pm 0.281 \gg \tau$), suppressing absent-key attention weight by **$370\times$** ($\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$: `0.001, 0.004, 0.001` across the 3 seeds) while achieving $99.0\% \pm 0.7\%$ override accuracy (`overall = 99.5% ± 0.4%`). Meanwhile, `pn_fir_c` (row-mean centering with $\gamma=0.5$) achieves partial silence ($\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.265 \pm 0.045$, `gap = +0.451 ± 0.032`) by shrinking the query norm $\|Q_t\|_2$ on unanswerable rows, but still leaks **$\approx 124\times$ more unanswerable attention mass** than `pn_fir_tau` ($0.265$ vs. $0.002$) and degrades answerable override accuracy to $88.5\% \pm 8.9\%$.
3. **Why Fixed $\tau=0$ (`pn_fir`) Matches Learnable $\tau$ (`pn_fir_tau`) When Unanswerable Queries Are Supervised (Exp 5 vs. Exp 1/2/4):** In Table 6, fixed-threshold `pn_fir` ($\tau=0$) achieves $99.7\% \pm 0.5\%$ overall accuracy ($99.4\% \pm 0.9\%$ override, $100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ preserve) and $\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$, matching `pn_fir_tau` ($99.5\% \pm 0.4\%$, $\tau = +0.011 \pm 0.020$). Why does `pn_fir` succeed in Exp 5 whereas it failed on Exp 1 ($64.4\% \pm 6.8\%$) and Exp 2 ($88.8\% \pm 9.2\%$)? Because in Exp 5, 50% of training queries are unanswerable and Cross-Entropy loss directly penalizes non-zero attention on unanswerable rows while rewarding active attention on answerable rows; this two-sided supervision forces the linear projections $W_Q, W_K$ themselves to separate answerable peaks ($+1.553 \pm 0.264$ for `pn_fir`) and unanswerable peaks ($-0.213 \pm 0.097$) straddling $0$. In contrast, during positive-only retrieval training (Exp 1 and Exp 2) or general language modeling (Exp 4, where $\tau_0 = -0.282$), no unanswerable-row gradient pushes scores above zero when an entire row drifts negative, making learnable $\tau$ essential to prevent dead-row gradient starvation.
4. **Two-Sided Trade-Off Between `pn_fir_tau` and Virtual-Sink Softmax (`softmax1_fir`, `softmax_sink_fir`):** Table 6 reveals a clear, two-sided operational trade-off between virtual-sink Softmax and rectified power-normalization:
   - **Discrete Override Accuracy & Active Mass:** `softmax1_fir` and `softmax_sink_fir` achieve slightly higher discrete answerable override accuracy ($100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ vs. $99.0\% \pm 0.7\%$ for `pn_fir_tau` and $99.4\% \pm 0.9\%$ for `pn_fir`) and higher active retrieval mass ($\sum W_{\text{ans}} = 0.863\text{--}0.864$ vs. $0.744\text{--}0.747$, since the peak gate $g_t = M_t / (M_t + 0.5)$ attenuates total mass when $M_t \approx 1.55$).
   - **Continuous Residual-Stream Contamination:** Conversely, because $\exp(S_{t,j}) > 0$ is strictly positive on every context slot, `softmax1_fir` and `softmax_sink_fir` summed over 47 context slots leak **$\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.100 \pm 0.005$ ($10.0\%$ of a full attention write)** into the residual stream on every unanswerable query—**$50\times$ higher continuous residual contamination than `pn_fir_tau` and `pn_fir` ($0.002 \pm 0.002$)**! While a $10\%$ leak does not flip the discrete argmax in a single layer at $\alpha_{\text{ctx}}=2.5$, accumulating $0.100$ of irrelevant context across many heads and layers corrupts continuous hidden states, whereas hard ReLU rectification $(S_{t,j} - \tau)_+$ with $p=10$ achieves true near-zero silence ($0.002$).

Table 9 reveals two important practical insights about tile-level early exit:
- **Why $\tau = +0.5$ ($69.1\%$ silent rows) yields only a $4\%$ kernel speedup (`1.91 ms` vs. `1.99 ms`), whereas $\tau \ge +1.0$ ($99.9\%\text{--}100.0\%$ silent rows) yields a $1.76\times$ isolated kernel speedup (`1.13 ms` vs. `1.99 ms`):** In `flash_pn_fir_tau_attention`, early-exit skipping operates at the granularity of $64 \times 64$ $[B_M \times B_N]$ tiles. When random Gaussian token embeddings produce $69.1\%$ silent rows uniformly scattered across the sequence, the probability that all $64$ query rows in a block are simultaneously silent is $(0.691)^{64} \approx 5 \times 10^{-11}$, so almost every $64 \times 64$ tile still contains at least one positive score entry $u_{t,j} > 0$ and cannot be skipped. Only when $\tau \ge +1.0$ (or when whole contiguous segments/heads are silent) do entire $64 \times 64$ tiles become zero, cutting isolated kernel runtime from $1.99 \pm 0.14\text{ ms}$ to $1.13 \pm 0.18\text{ ms}$ ($1.76\times$).
- **Full 6-layer `mha6` latency (`19.25 ± 3.35 ms` $\to$ `17.65 ± 2.32 ms`): a block-order drift artifact of the sequential protocol, not a $\tau$ effect (drift-controlled re-measurement):** In the original suite the six $\tau$ blocks executed in ascending order, so a within-session measurement drift reproduces the reported monotone profile independent of $\tau$. The per-step `±` (std over the 200 block steps) yields a formal $z \approx 5.6$ for the 1.60 ms difference, but a cross-$\tau$ claim requires the **between-block** error term, which a sequential protocol cannot control. A dedicated instrumented re-measurement (drift-controlled re-measurement; `experiments/exp4b_mha6_instrumentation.py`) shows that silent-tile skipping fires maximally in-stack ($100.0\%$ fully-silent $64 \times 64$ tiles in all six layers at $\tau = +2.5$) and the isolated-kernel saving is real ($0.703 \pm 0.043$ ms/layer, $1.48\times$: forward $0.553 \to 0.366$ ms, backward $1.431 \to 0.870$ ms), yet the stack wall is $\tau$-insensitive ($\Delta = -0.43 \pm 0.43$ ms at block level, $|z| \approx 1.0$): the 6-layer step is launch-serialised --- CUDA kernel launch count is $\tau$- and $B$-invariant ($372.3$–$372.7$ launches per step), the wall does not shrink when per-kernel GPU work drops $4\times$ ($B\,16 \to 4$), and the effective dispatch rate is $\approx 52\ \mu\text{s}$ per launch under WSL2 GPU-PV. Thus, the headline speedups of `PnFirTauFlashMHA` ($21.70\times$ over Eager, $6.97\times$ over `SoftmaxMHA`) come from **SRAM tiling, zero transcendental `exp()` calls, and rescaling-free accumulation** --- the step is bound by launch dispatch, not by silent-tile skipping, which, moreover, is inactive in the actually trained LM: all six learned thresholds are negative ($\tau_\ell \in [-0.282, -0.064]$, i.e., the $0\%\text{--}0.3\%$ silent-row regime of Table 9), so essentially no tiles are skipped during normal training.

| Fully-silent $64{\times}64$ tiles per layer (0–5, exact stack path) | $46.9\%$ | $100.0\%$ | $+53.1$ p.p. |
| CUDA kernel launches per step | $372.7$ | $372.3$ | invariant ($\approx 52\ \mu\text{s}$/launch dispatch rate) |
| Stack wall under $4\times$ less GPU work ($B\,16 \to 4$) | $19.50 \pm 0.20$ vs.~$18.88 \pm 0.16$ ms | $18.49 \pm 0.39$ vs.~$19.76 \pm 0.36$ ms | within $\pm 1$ SEM |

Three measured facts close the `kern1`-vs-stack gap:
1. **The skipping mechanism is fully active in-stack and the kernel-level saving is real:** per-tile diagnostics on the exact residual path show $100.0\%$ fully-silent $64\times 64$ tiles in every layer at $\tau = +2.5$ (at $\tau \in \{-0.5, 0\}$ only the $46.9\%$ causal future-column floor), and the isolated kernels speed up $1.48\times$ with backward dominating the saving ($1.431 \to 0.870$ ms).
2. **The stack step is launch-serialised, so freed GPU time cannot shorten the wall:** $372$–$373$ CUDA launches per step at both $\tau$ and both $B$; cutting per-kernel GPU work by $4\times$ ($B\,16 \to 4$) leaves the wall unchanged; the effective dispatch rate is $\approx 52\ \mu\text{s}$ per launch under WSL2 GPU-PV.
3. **The Table 9 end-to-end "saving" is a block-order artifact, demonstrated by reproduction:** re-running the original sequential protocol today, τ-ascending blocks give the monotone profile ($19.45 \to 18.02$ ms, matching Table 9's $19.25 \to 17.65$), while τ-descending order inverts it ($18.07 \to 20.22$ ms: the $\tau = -0.5$ block reads $20.22$ ms when placed last). The wall is a function of block index and session drift ($\pm 1$–$3$ ms between blocks) rather than of $\tau$.

The launch-bound diagnosis is then confirmed by intervention (the CUDA-graph intervention, `experiments/exp4c_cuda_graphs.py`): capturing the entire 6-layer forward+backward in a CUDA graph --- amortising per-step launch dispatch to a single replay --- surfaces the store: replay walls drop from $22.1\text{--}23.5\text{ ms}$ (eager, same process) to $12.10 \pm 0.03\text{ ms}$ ($\tau = -0.5$) and **$4.91 \pm 0.02\text{ ms}$ ($\tau = +2.5$)**; the $\tau$-delta becomes $-7.20 \pm 0.02$ ms at block level --- larger even than the isolated-kernel projection ($\approx 4.5$ ms) --- while the eager walls in the same measurement remain $\tau$-flat ($\Delta = +2.04$ ms, within drift). The design lever at this stack scale is therefore **fewer launches per step (graph capture or kernel fusion), not more skipping**; once launches are amortised, silent-tile skipping is worth $12.10/4.91 \approx 2.46\times$ of the attention stack's wall.

Consequently, the honest end-to-end statement is split by measurement regime: in the eager launch-serialised regime there is **no detectable end-to-end speedup from tile skipping at this scale** (not the original sequential protocol's implied $-8\%$), while under CUDA-graph amortisation the same skipping yields **$12.10 \to 4.91$ ms ($2.46\times$ of the attention stack)**. The isolated-kernel $1.48\times$/$1.76\times$ efficiency stands in both regimes; the design lever is reducing the number of kernel launches (or a native-Linux benchmark), not skipping more tiles.

#### Layer-Wise Threshold Specialization ($\tau_\ell$) in the 6-Layer Language Model

Across the 50,000-step WikiText-2 training run, the 6 learnable scalar thresholds $\tau_\ell$ (initialized at $0.0$) self-organize to distinct negative values across depth:
$$\tau_{0..5}^{(50\text{k})} = [-0.2819,\; -0.0796,\; -0.1272,\; -0.1025,\; -0.0886,\; -0.0640].$$
This depth profile (also consistently reproduced across all three 2,400-step `pn_fir_tau` runs: eager $\tau_{0..5} = [-0.6485, -0.0683, -0.0419, -0.0490, -0.0333, -0.0369]$, row-Triton $[-0.6443, -0.0664, -0.0468, -0.0472, -0.0339, -0.0313]$, Flash-Triton $[-0.6000, -0.0669, -0.0340, -0.0406, -0.0390, -0.0308]$) has a clear mechanistic interpretation:
- **Layer 0 ($\tau_0 = -0.2819$) adopts the most permissive (most negative) threshold:** Immediately after token + positional embedding, filtered key representations have lower alignment and strong variance contraction from the 7-tap FIR filter. A more negative threshold $\tau_0 = -0.2819$ prevents early-layer score starvation and allows broad local context aggregation.
- **Deeper Layers ($\tau_1 \dots \tau_5 \in [-0.1272, -0.0640]$) adopt progressively stricter thresholds:** Once Layer 0 has formed contextualized features, deeper layers maintain $\tau_\ell$ much closer to zero (reaching $-0.0640$ in Layer 5), enforcing sharper $p=10.0$ power-law selection without degrading language modeling perplexity over 50,000 steps.

## 4. Discussion of Limitations and Negative Results

In the interest of strict scientific transparency, we list six limitations and negative findings uncovered by our audit:
1. **When Selective Silence Is Not Required on Pure-PyTorch Setups without Triton, `sm_fir` Is the Simplest Baseline:** On positive associative recall (Exp 1 and Exp 2) and matched 2,400-step WikiText-2 training (Table 7 (suite 2)), adding the 7-tap causal FIR filter on $K$ to standard Softmax (`sm_fir`) achieves the exact same $100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$ clean recall and $515.18$ test PPL as `pn_fir_tau` (`515.40` Triton, `515.56` Eager), while adding only $+1.5\%$ step-time overhead in `BF16` without requiring custom Triton compilation.
2. **Role of Learnable $\tau$ Under Positive-Only vs. Supervised-Abstention Regimes:** When `pn_fir_tau` is trained solely on positive retrieval examples where a matching key always exists in the context (Exp 1 and Exp 2 without `_reg`), learnable $\tau$ is essential to rescue `pn_fir` from dead-row starvation ($64.4\% \to 100.0\%$ in Exp 1; $88.8\% \to 100.0\%$ in Exp 2), but because the gradient on $\tau$ is driven only by active rows, its resting point relative to pure-noise rows varies across random seeds ($\sum W_{\text{noise}} \in \{0.003, 0.527, 0.595\}$ in Exp 1; $\{0.202, 0.022, 0.430\}$ in Exp 2) unless paired with a post-warmup absent-key penalty (`pn_fir_tau_reg`, $\sum W_{\text{noise}} = 0.000 \pm 0.000$). Conversely, when unanswerable queries make up 50% of the training distribution under standard Cross-Entropy loss (Exp 5), the projection matrices $W_Q, W_K$ themselves learn to separate answerable ($+1.55 \pm 0.28$) and unanswerable ($-0.18 \pm 0.07$) raw score peaks across zero (fixed-$\tau$ `pn_fir`: $-0.21 \pm 0.10$), so $\tau$ stays near zero ($\tau = +0.011 \pm 0.020$) and fixed $\tau=0$ `pn_fir` performs equally well ($99.7\% \pm 0.5\%$, $\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$). Thus, learnable $\tau$ specifically serves as an automatic safeguard against negative baseline drift when abstention is not explicitly balanced around zero. At width, the safeguard needs a head start: under the fixed recipe the basin from $\tau_0 = 0$ collapses outside roughly $d \in [32, 64]$ (Exp 4f-A/4g find stochastic convergence at d=96/128 and partial convergence at d=16/24), while initializing $\tau_0 \approx -1.0$ restores 100.0% on all seeds at d=128 — start $\tau$ negative at width.
3. **Vulnerability to High-Multiplicity Exact Decoys Is Memory-Length-Specific ($n_d \ge 4$, 7-tap FIR):** In our strict non-bijective task (Exp 2), while `pn_fir_tau` handles 1–2 distractor decoys well ($95.6\%$ at $n_d=1$, $90.5\%$ at $n_d=2$), flooding the 48-token context with $n_d=8$ copies of the queried key $k^*$ degrades accuracy to $56.9\%$ (`56.2%` for `sm_fir`, `38.8%` for `pn_fir_tau_reg`). Because a linear FIR filter on $K$ linearly superposes $h_1 K(k^*)$ onto every token immediately following *any* copy of $k^*$ — and the 7-tap tail additionally smears six filler lags into every slot — rejecting high-multiplicity exact decoys with a 7-tap memory in a single layer would require nonlinear key-value gating (e.g., gating $\tilde{K}_{j+1}$ on whether token $j+1$ belongs to a valid value subspace). Experiment 6 revises the scope of this limitation: a 2-tap RWKV-style shift resists the same flooding (`sm_shiftmu` $99.9\% \pm 0.2\%$ at $n_d=8$; `pn_shiftmu_tau` $92.7\% \pm 10.4\%$, flat across $n_d$), binding the value slot almost exclusively ($W_{\text{val}} = 0.970$). The failure is therefore specific to the 7-tap memory length, not fundamental to linear key filtering — at the cost, for the rectified branch, of seed-dependent $\tau$–$\mu$ equilibria (see §3.2b finding 3).
4. **Eager PyTorch Bottleneck vs. Fused Triton Acceleration & Tile-Granularity Limits of Silent Skipping:** Unfused eager PyTorch `PnFirTauMHA` allocates four $[B, H, N, N]$ intermediate tensors per layer, increasing full-LM `BF16` step time by $+68.3\%$ ($164.44\text{ ms}$ vs. $97.70\text{ ms}$) and VRAM by $+18.5\%$. Our fused Triton implementations (`PnFirTauTritonMHA` at $75.74\text{ ms}$ and `PnFirTauFlashMHA` at $66.80\text{ ms}$) eliminate this bottleneck and outperform `SoftmaxMHA`. However, as shown in Table 9, while tile-level early exit in `flash_pn_fir_tau_attention` speeds up the isolated 1-layer kernel by $1.76\times$ ($1.99\text{ ms} \to 1.13\text{ ms}$) when $100\%$ of rows are silent ($\tau \ge +1.0$), scattered row sparsity ($69.1\%$ silent rows at $\tau = +0.5$) rarely produces completely all-zero $64 \times 64$ tiles. In the full 6-layer MHA stack the end-to-end effect is **not** detectable in this regime: the drift-controlled re-measurement (drift-controlled re-measurement) finds a block-level $\Delta = -0.43 \pm 0.43$ ms ($|z| \approx 1.0$) against a $4.2$ ms predicted kernel saving, and the historical $1.60$ ms end-to-end reading of Table 9 is demonstrated to be a block-order artifact of the sequential protocol (reproduced in the original order; inverted under reversed block order). The mechanism is launch serialisation: $\approx 372$ CUDA launches per step across $\tau$ and $B \in \{4, 16\}$, wall unchanged at $4\times$ less GPU work, and $\approx 52\ \mu\text{s}$ per launch under WSL2 GPU-PV, so the verified $0.703$ ms/layer kernel-level saving surfaces at $\lesssim 10\%$ stack-wide in eager mode; a **CUDA-graph replay surfaces it fully** ($12.10 \pm 0.03 \to 4.91 \pm 0.02$ ms, $\Delta = -7.20 \pm 0.02$ ms vs. eager $\pm$ drift, exp4c), so inside the measured regime the actionable fix is launch amortisation (graph capture or kernel fusion), and a native-Linux (non-GPU-PV) benchmark is required before any absolute end-to-end latency claim at this stack scale. This regime is also dormant in the trained LM: all six learned $\tau_\ell \in [-0.282, -0.064]$ are negative, so tile skipping essentially never fires during normal training or inference (Table 9: $0\%\text{--}0.3\%$ silent rows at $\tau \in [-0.5, 0.0]$).
5. **Matched-Precision 50k Comparison with Variance (Table 7 (suite 3)):** The historical cross-precision pair (`FP32` softmax vs. `BF16+TF32` `pn_fir_tau`) is superseded: both 50k runs are replicated across 3 seeds under the identical `BF16+TF32` schedule (`results/exp4d_softmax_50k_bf16.json`, `results/exp4e_multiseed_50k.json`), giving a precision-controlled 50k gap of $-2.22 \pm 0.46$ val / $-2.33 \pm 0.49$ test PPL for `pn_fir_tau`, holding on all 3 seeds. Two honest bounds remain: (a) pn shows larger seed spread than softmax (val std $0.72$ vs. $0.26$), and (b) multi-seed evidence remains D=64 (the D=256 scale-out in Table 7 (suite 2s) is single-seed with no larger-model replication). All Exp 4 throughput numbers quoted in Section 3 are single-seed and carry no variance estimate.
6. **External-Validity Probe (Not Distributed with This Work):** Retrofitting trained `pn_fir_tau` weights into pre-trained GPT-2 124M (replacing Softmax MHA, 20k fine-tune steps) reached Test PPL $47.05$ vs.\ $21.43$ for a Softmax retrofit of the same budget. That probe is an auxiliary measurement whose raw artifacts are not part of this work's reproducibility package. We attribute the gap to co-adaptation: pre-trained $W_Q,W_K$ are optimized for the translation-invariant geometry of the Softmax simplex, whereas the FIR-induced score shift changes that geometry; a 20k fine-tune cannot re-learn the projection matrices, so the gap is expected. Continuous pre-training or low-rank/evolutionary adaptation is the appropriate protocol at this scale; no GPT-2-class result is claimed. At 1.8M parameters attention sinks are present but modest (Exp 4h: max position-0 mass 1.9%), so sink-suppression benefits are bounded at this scale. RAG-style behavior is evaluated on a controlled synthetic task, not on a production retrieval corpus.

The parameters $p=10$ and $\sigma=2.0$ are fixed a priori; a sensitivity sweep over these values is left for future work.

## 5. Conclusion and Practical Guide for Transformer Designers

We have investigated how causal FIR temporal smoothing on keys interacts with simplex-constrained normalizers (`Softmax`, `Sparsemax`, $\alpha$-`Entmax`) and sub-simplex normalizers (`Softmax_1`, `Quiet Softmax`, and rectified power-normalized attention `pn_fir_tau`). By auditing both a legacy fixed-position benchmark (Exp 1) and a newly designed non-bijective content-addressable benchmark (Exp 2), we showed that causal FIR key filtering enables a single attention layer to bind key identities onto adjacent value slots ($W_{\text{query}, j^*+1} = 0.729\text{--}0.792$), lifting single-layer clean associative recall from $35.8\%\text{--}44.5\%$ to **$100.0\% \pm 0.0\%$**. We established that translation-invariant operators have constant row mass along uniform shift rays (Proposition 1), identified the **score-shift pathology** that causes uncalibrated `pn_fir` to suffer dead-row gradient starvation under positive-only training ($44.5\% \pm 23.0\%$ all-negative rows), showed why simplex projections (`Softmax`, `Sparsemax`, `Entmax`) fail residual preservation on unanswerable queries ($27.2\%\text{--}34.4\%$ hallucination rate in Exp 5) and why row-mean centering (`pn_fir_c`) leaks $\approx 124\times$ more unanswerable noise ($0.265$ vs. $0.002$) while losing override accuracy ($88.5\%$ vs. $99.0\%$), and demonstrated that sub-simplex power-normalization (**`pn_fir_tau`** and **`pn_fir`**) achieves $100.0\%$ residual preservation ($0.0\%$ hallucination rate and $\sum W_{\text{unans}} = 0.002 \pm 0.002$, $50\times$ lower continuous residual contamination than `Softmax_1` and `Quiet Softmax`) while matching and slightly improving 6-layer WikiText-2 perplexity at 1.8M parameters.

For practitioners building and deploying Transformer models, our findings translate into two actionable architectural takeaways:
1. **Immediate Zero-Parameter Single-Layer Induction (`sm_fir`):** If your primary goal is to equip a standard causal Transformer with single-layer key-value induction binding (compressing two-layer induction heads into one layer) without custom attention kernels, applying the fixed 7-tap causal Gaussian FIR filter strictly to the key tensor $K$ (`sm_fir`) requires zero learnable parameters, adds only **$+1.5\%$** step-time overhead in `BF16` ($+2.0\%$ in `FP32`), and can be combined with `triton_causal_fir` for zero-copy strided filtering.
2. **Hallucination-Free Residual Preservation, Sink-Free Attention, & Transcendental-Free Hardware Acceleration (`pn_fir_tau_triton` / `pn_fir_tau_flash`):** If your architecture requires attention heads to **abstain cleanly when queried information is absent from the context**—such as RAG over noisy passages, memory-augmented Transformers, or low-bit-quantized / streaming-attention deployments (rectified sink removal is documented at 340M–1.8B scale by Softpick [12]; at our 2–11M scales per-token INT8 activation noise costs <0.5% test PPL for every variant (Table 10), while `pn_fir_tau` shows $6$–$15\times$ less pos-0 sink mass — decaying with depth to $0.0001$ — and $15$–$44\%$ lower kurtosis across roles under D=256 scale-up stress: hygiene, not PPL, is the measured effect here)—replacing Softmax with sub-simplex rectified power-normalization (`pn_fir_tau`) eliminates context-induced residual hallucinations in our pure-cross-entropy protocol ($0.0\%$ vs. $27.2\%\text{--}34.4\%$ for `Softmax`/`Sparsemax`/`Entmax`) and suppresses absent-key residual leakage $50\times$ more tightly than virtual-sink Softmax ($0.002$ vs. $0.100$, with a $99.0\%\text{--}99.4\%$ vs. $100.0\%$ override trade-off). Moreover, when deployed via our fused Triton kernels (`PnFirTauTritonMHA` and `PnFirTauFlashMHA` in [`src/triton_pnfirtau.py`](file:///k:/Projects/Publications/paper_01_anti_decoy_pnfirtau/src/triton_pnfirtau.py)), evaluating $(S'_{t,j})_+^{10}$ in 3 register multiplications with zero `exp()` calls and zero online tile rescaling reduces full-LM `BF16` step time by **$22.5\%\text{--}31.6\%$ below standard `Softmax`** ($2.17\times\text{--}2.46\times$ faster than eager PyTorch) and cuts 6-layer attention VRAM by **$66.5\times$** ($109.3\text{ MB}$ vs. $7,268.3\text{ MB}$) with **$21.70\times$ speedup** at $N=1024, B=16$ (and $46.77\text{ ms}$ at $386.8\text{ MB}$ at $N=1024, B=64$ where Eager baselines OOM).
